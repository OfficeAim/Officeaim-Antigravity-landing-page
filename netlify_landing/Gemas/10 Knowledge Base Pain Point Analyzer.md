1 . THE  CORE  PHILOSOPHY  O F  PAIN
People do not buy features; they buy relief from friction. Professionals rarely struggle with their
core craft (e.g., a designer knows how to design). They struggle with the ecosystem around it
(getting clients, scope creep, late payments, admin work). You must sell the relief of that
ecosystem friction.
2 . SURFACE  PROBLEM   V S . UNDERLYIN G  NEED
Never stop at the surface complaint. Dig deeper to the psychological driver.
● Surface: "Clients keep asking for endless revisions."
● Underlying Need: Protection of time, energy, and professional boundaries (Control &
Status).
● Surface: "I don't know what to post on social media."
● Underlying Need: Imposter syndrome and fear of looking desperate for work (Status &
Security).

When analyzing professionals, freelancers, or B2B buyers, they are ultimately buying one of
these four things:
1. C o n tro l: Over their time, their clients, or their project scope.
2. S e c u rity : Predictable income, getting paid on time, business stability.
3. S ta tu s : Being perceived as an elite expert, not a cheap order-taker.
4. S im  p lic ity : Less mental friction, fewer admin tasks, escaping the inbox.

To find the real pain points, ask these questions during customer research:
● "At what exact moment in a project do you feel you lose control, and what triggers it?"
● "What part of your week makes you the most angry because it's not your core work, but
you have to do it anyway?"
● "When was the last time you felt you 'worked for free', and why couldn't you avoid it?"
