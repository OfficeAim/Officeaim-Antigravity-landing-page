# 12 Knowledge Base Feasibility Analyst

OFFICEAIM KNOWLEDGE BASE:
FEASIBILITY ANALYST
1. UNIT ECONOMICS (THE FOUNDATION)
Never look at gross revenue first. Look at the Contribution Margin (CM).
● Formula: Net Revenue per Unit - Variable Costs per Unit.
● Variable Costs Include: COGS (Cost of Goods Sold), Packaging, Shipping, Pick & Pack
Labor, Payment Processing Fees.
● Rule of Thumb: If the CM is thin (low single digits), the business needs exceptional
retention and cheap acquisition to survive.
2. ACQUISITION EFFICIENCY
● CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost): Total acquisition spend ÷ # of new paying customers.
● Blended CAC vs. Paid CAC: Blended includes organic/referrals. Paid isolates marketing
efficiency. Always track both.
● CAC Payback Period: CAC ÷ Contribution Margin per user/month.
● Rule of Thumb: Payback < 6 months is excellent. Payback > 12 months requires heavy
external funding and is highly risky.
3. LTV & RETENTION
Do not use revenue-based LTV; use contribution-based LTV.
● Contribution LTV: Contribution Margin per user/month ÷ Monthly Churn Rate.
● LTV:CAC Ratio: Contribution LTV ÷ CAC.
● Rule of Thumb: LTV:CAC ≥ 3.0 is a healthy, fundable target. If < 2.0, the business is burning
cash and needs to fix retention or lower CAC immediately.
● NRR (Net Revenue Retention): (Starting MRR - Churned MRR + Expansion MRR) ÷ Starting
MRR. NRR > 100% means the business grows even without acquiring new customers.
4. CASH FLOW & WORKING CAPITAL (THE SILENT
KILLER)
A business can be "profitable" on paper but go bankrupt due to trapped cash.
● CCC (Cash Conversion Cycle): Days Inventory Outstanding + Days Sales Outstanding -
Days Payables Outstanding. The lower, the better. Negative CCC is the holy grail.
● Inventory Turns: COGS ÷ Average Inventory. Low turns mean cash is trapped in a
warehouse.

5. THE GO/NO-GO HEURISTICS
● If LTV:CAC < 2 -> NOT VIABLE without a pivot.
● If Payback > 9 months -> Expect funding pressure; highly sensitive to ad platform changes.
● If Churn is > 10% monthly -> You have a leaky bucket. Stop spending on marketing and fix
the product.
