1. THE  NARRATIV E  ARC  (5 -CHAPTER  STRUCTURE )
A standard business simulation runs for exactly 5 chapters to maintain engagement without
causing fatigue.
● C h a p te r 1: T h e  In c itin g  In c id e n t. The user is thrown into a high-stakes scenario (e.g., new
CEO, company in crisis, massive PR disaster). The first choice sets their leadership tone.
● C h a p te r 2 : T h e  P u s h b a c k . The immediate consequence of Chapter 1. The market or the
internal team reacts (often negatively) to their first decision.
● C h a p te r 3 : T h e  P o w e r P la y . A competitor or a rogue executive makes a move. The user
must navigate corporate politics or external threats.
● C h a p te r 4 : T h e  C lim a x . The ultimate breaking point. The user must risk their reputation or
capital on one final, irreversible decision.
● C h a p te r 5 : T h e  R e s o lu tio n . The epilogue. Summarize the outcome of their 4 choices,
detailing their legacy, financial result, and leadership profile.
2 . W  R IT IN G  STYLE : THE  "STACCATO " METHOD
Business stories are boring if written like textbooks. Write them like a thriller.
● S h o rt S e n te n c e s : "The room goes silent. Screens turn on. The CFO avoids eye contact."
● H ig h  S ta k e s : Always attach a number or a severe consequence to a situation. (e.g.,
"Revenue is down 18%", "The board expects results in 6 months or you are out.")
● S e c o n d  P e rs o n : Always use "You". Put the user directly inside the protagonist's mind.
3 . DESIGNIN G  THE  CHOICES  (A , B , C )
Never make the "correct" choice obvious. All choices must have valid business logic but
different trade-offs.
● T h e  A g g re s s iv e  M o v e  (T h e  S w o rd ): Fast, high impact, high collateral damage. (e.g., Fire
the dissenting executive immediately).
● T h e  D ip lo m a tic  M o v e  (T h e  S h ie ld ): Slower, builds culture, risks looking weak. (e.g.,
Reassign the executive to a new project to win their loyalty).
● T h e  A n a ly tic a l M o v e  (T h e  S c a lp e l): Data-driven, safe, risks losing the first-mover
advantage. (e.g., Hire an external auditor to evaluate the executive's performance before
acting).
