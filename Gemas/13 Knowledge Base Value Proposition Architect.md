# 13 Knowledge Base Value Proposition Architect

OFFICEAIM KNOWLEDGE BASE: VALUE
PROPOSITION ARCHITECT
1. THE UVP CORE SENTENCE FORMULA
Every defensible value proposition must fit this exact structure to ensure absolute clarity:
● For [Ideal Customer]
● who [Top Job-to-be-Done + context]
● our [Product name/type]
● is a [Category customers recognize]
● that [#1 measurable outcome / benefit]
● by [how it works in plain language].
● Unlike [main alternative: competitors or the status quo]
● we [unique mechanism + why it’s credible/defensible].
2. THE MESSAGE STACK (WEBSITE COPY)
A UVP is useless if it can't be read on a homepage. Translate the core sentence into the
Message Stack:
● Headline: The ultimate outcome in 8–12 words.
● Subhead: Who it’s for + how it delivers that outcome.
● 3 Bullets: (1) Pain reliever, (2) Gain creator, (3) Proof/Credibility.
● Proof: Metric, customer type, integration list, or security badge.
3. THE 3 POSITIONING ANGLES
When defining a product, map out 3 distinct ways to position it:
1. Alternative A (Eliminate the Biggest Pain): Focuses on the root cause of why the
customer is failing today. Best for mature markets with high frustration.
2. Alternative B (Create the Biggest Gain): Focuses on ROI, predictable revenue, or
massive growth. Best for selling to leadership/executives (high ACV).
3. Alternative C (The Differentiation Wedge): Focuses on a unique UX, integration, or niche
feature. Best when you have killer integrations and want a memorable, specific entry point.
4. THE SCORING FILTER
Evaluate every UVP angle based on these 5 criteria (1-5 scale):
1. Urgency: Is the pain expensive, embarrassing, or risky right now?
2. Clarity: Will a buyer “get it” in 5 seconds without deep explanation?
3. Differentiation: Can competitors claim the exact same thing?
4. Proofability: Can we show evidence quickly (e.g., a 10-second demo GIF)?

5. Adoption Friction: How hard is the switching effort, data migration, or behavior change?
5. JOBS-TO-BE-DONE (JTBD)
Never just list features. Understand the "Jobs" the customer is hiring the product to do:
● Functional: The literal task (e.g., track pipeline).
● Social: How they want to be perceived (e.g., look organized to leadership).
● Emotional: How they want to feel (e.g., reduce anxiety, feel in control).
