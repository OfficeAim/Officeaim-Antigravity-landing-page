# Marketing Psychology

Source: https://skills.sh/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/marketing-psychology

## When to Apply
Reference these principles when:
- Writing copy for landing pages, pricing, or feature sections
- Designing CTA buttons and their surrounding context
- Structuring pricing tiers and feature lists
- Adding social proof, urgency, or trust signals
- Reviewing why a page isn't converting

## Core Psychological Principles

### 1. Loss Aversion (Highest Impact)
People feel losses ~2x more than equivalent gains. Frame copy around what they *lose* by not acting, not just what they gain.

- ❌ "Get access to 6 AI agents"
- ✅ "Without OfficeAIM, you're doing manually what 6 AI specialists should handle"

### 2. Social Proof
People follow what others do. The more specific, the stronger the signal.
- **Logos**: "Used by teams at [Company]"
- **Numbers**: "Join 2,400+ students"
- **Testimonials**: Named, photo, specific outcome
- **Activity signals**: "47 people signed up today"

### 3. Authority & Expertise
People trust experts. Establish credibility early.
- Credentials, partner logos, media mentions
- Specific technical language that demonstrates mastery
- "Built on Google Gemini 2.5" signals tech credibility

### 4. Scarcity & Urgency
Limited availability creates action. Must be genuine.
- Time-limited offers ("Ends Friday")
- Seat limits ("Only 12 spots left in cohort")
- Feature gating ("Idea Cloner — Unlocked in Módulo 2")

### 5. Reciprocity
Give first; people feel compelled to give back.
- Free tier agents = value before payment
- Free Gem agents on gems.html = no-login preview
- Free tools reduce psychological barrier to signing up

### 6. Anchoring
The first number seen sets the reference point.
- Show the highest plan first on pricing (or the Pro plan featured)
- Strike through original price
- "€49/month" feels cheap after seeing what's included

### 7. Cognitive Ease
Familiar, easy-to-read things feel more true and trustworthy.
- Simple language
- Clean design with enough whitespace
- Chunked information (not walls of text)
- Progressive disclosure (show basics first, details on demand)

### 8. The Endowment Effect
People value things more once they feel ownership.
- Personalized onboarding ("Your AI board is ready")
- Dashboard greeting using user name
- "Activate Your Agents" (not "Sign up")

### 9. Commitment & Consistency
Small commitments lead to larger ones.
- Free tier → engagement → Pro upgrade
- Progress bars in curriculum create commitment to completion
- "You've completed 2 of 6 modules" leverages this

### 10. The Peak-End Rule
People judge experiences by their peak moment and the end, not the average.
- Make the first agent interaction memorable (Gema greeting)
- Make the upgrade moment feel special
- Make the session-end save feel rewarding (Neural Vault)

## Pricing Page Psychology Checklist
- [ ] Is the recommended plan visually dominant?
- [ ] Is the free plan listed first or with a clear visual anchor?
- [ ] Does the Pro plan badge ("Most Popular") add social proof?
- [ ] Are the features phrased as outcomes, not just capabilities?
- [ ] Is price anchoring used (Plus shown before Pro)?
- [ ] Is there an FAQ that addresses the top 3 objections?
- [ ] Is "cancel anytime" clearly visible near paid CTAs?
